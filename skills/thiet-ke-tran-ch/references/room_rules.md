# Room Rules

## 1. Living room

### Design axis
Use the furniture composition as the primary reference:
- sofa;
- TV wall;
- coffee table;
- connection to dining area.

Furniture axis generally has priority over geometric room center.

### Lighting
- decorative light, where specified, follows the center of the furniture composition;
- downlights shall form a coherent grid / sequence around the furniture layout;
- minimum light-to-light spacing = 1200 mm;
- nearest light row to wall >= 500 mm;
- approximately 600 mm is preferred where practical;
- avoid placing downlights directly against TV wall or curtain pelmet unless intentionally required.

### HVAC
- linear grilles should align with room / façade / furniture geometry;
- avoid direct uncomfortable supply air toward long-stay seating;
- return air generally favors the inner side of the room where technically appropriate.

### Fire safety
- sprinkler and detector positions follow technical requirements;
- after placing them, adjust lower-priority lighting to recover composition.

### Access panels
- avoid the visual center of the living room;
- prefer perimeter / secondary ceiling zones where service access allows.

---

## 2. Dining room / dining zone

### Primary axis
Dining table center and table long axis.

### Pendant
- pendant point shall align with the dining-table center;
- if table shifts, the pendant follows the intended final table position rather than room center.

### General lighting
- coordinate downlights around the dining pendant;
- maintain >= 1200 mm between general lights;
- avoid visually crowding pendant, sprinkler and detector.

---

## 3. Bedroom

### Primary axis
Bed centerline is normally the primary architectural ceiling axis.

### Lighting
- arrange downlights symmetrically relative to bed axis where possible;
- do not place strong general downlight directly over the pillow / head zone where avoidable;
- minimum light-to-light spacing = 1200 mm;
- nearest light row to wall >= 500 mm; ~600 mm preferred.

### Wardrobe
Wardrobe footprint is a strict ceiling-device exclusion zone.
No light or ceiling-mounted device shall be placed within this footprint.

### HVAC
- do not discharge supply air directly toward pillow / head zone;
- preferred airflow is along the side of the bed or toward the lower / central room zone;
- keep return-air location technically effective and visually aligned.

### Fire safety
- locate detector / sprinkler according to technical requirements;
- avoid unnecessary clustering with lights.

### Access panel
- avoid above bed and especially above headboard where practical;
- prefer entrance-side / secondary ceiling zone.

---

## 4. Kitchen

### Primary references
- kitchen cabinet run;
- countertop;
- cooking position;
- circulation.

### Lighting
Functional task lighting has priority.
- light the countertop / preparation zone;
- do not rely solely on geometric room center;
- maintain general-light spacing rules where multiple downlights are used.

### Fire detection
Where the approved fire strategy calls for a heat detector, coordinate it with the cooking zone but do not place it directly over a heat source unless the technical design requires it.

### Sprinkler
Do not reposition purely for appearance.
Coordinate around hood, cabinets, soffits and other obstructions.

### Exhaust
Align exhaust device with the actual kitchen / hood system.

### Access panel
Locate near equipment requiring service but avoid positions blocked by tall cabinetry.

---

## 5. WC / bathroom

This room has a special alignment rule.

### Primary axes
Ceiling devices shall be aligned intentionally with sanitary-fixture axes:
- wash-basin axis;
- WC axis;
- shower axis;
- bathtub axis where applicable.

Do not use one generic room-center grid if it ignores sanitary fixtures.

### Basin / mirror zone
- mirror light shall align with the basin / mirror centerline;
- ceiling light serving the basin zone should align with the basin axis where practical.

### WC zone
- ceiling device serving the WC zone should align with the WC centerline or the clear functional zone around it, subject to technical requirements.

### Shower zone
- ceiling light / relevant device should align with the shower centerline or defined shower zone;
- all electrical equipment must follow the applicable wet-area / IP / electrical requirements supplied by the project.

### General order
The ceiling should read as an intentional composition tied to the fixture layout:
`BASIN AXIS — WC AXIS — SHOWER AXIS`
rather than a random device distribution.

### Access panel
WC / bathroom is often a suitable secondary location for maintenance access when technically appropriate.
The panel must remain openable and must not clash with sanitary fixtures, shower screen or door swing.

### Wardrobe / cabinet
Any full-height bathroom cabinet or tall storage element shall also be checked as a no-device zone when it reaches the ceiling or obstructs device operation / access.

---

## 6. Multipurpose room

Because furniture may change:
- geometric room axis normally takes priority;
- use a neutral, flexible lighting grid;
- avoid overfitting the ceiling to one temporary furniture arrangement;
- still respect any fixed wardrobe / joinery exclusion zone.

---

## 7. Corridor / entrance

- establish a clear longitudinal axis;
- align downlights along the circulation axis;
- maintain >= 1200 mm light spacing where multiple lights are used, unless only a single fixture is appropriate;
- keep access panels toward secondary zones where possible;
- coordinate closely with smoke detector / sprinkler because corridors are often device-dense.

---

## 8. Loggia

- use appropriate exterior / semi-exterior light type from project specification;
- align lighting with door / loggia geometry;
- coordinate with outdoor AC units, drainage, drying racks and service access;
- do not force an indoor downlight grid onto the loggia.
